Executive summary

Money Machine has six responsibilities:

  • Trade: scan markets, grade setups, size positions, manage open trades.
  • Protect: enforce per-trade risk, daily-loss, exposure, and cooldown limits.
  • Record: keep an auditable SQLite journal of scans, signals, trades, equity, jobs, and logs.
  • Control: expose a dashboard, JSON API, and Telegram command plane.
  • Research: run an approximate historical backtest of the same strategy.
  • Operate: schedule scans, back up the database, report health, and send alerts.

The strategy is multi-timeframe trend continuation using Smart Money Concepts (SMC) and price action. It is not high-frequency trading, arbitrage, grid, martingale, or an AI/LLM decision engine.

Key conclusions

  • The system has a coherent trend + pullback + trigger hypothesis.
  • Its own published sample backtest was negative after costs, so the edge is not proven.
  • The best next research step is regime and volatility filtering plus better exits, not a calendar-only “buy Monday / sell at month-end” rule.
  • Month-end and weekday effects exist in some historical crypto samples, but they are weak and unstable. Use them as risk and liquidity context, not as a standalone profit engine.

Scan loop

APScheduler triggers a scan every SCAN_INTERVAL_SECONDS (20 seconds by default). The scan runs in a worker thread so network calls never block the API or Telegram. For each configured symbol:

  1. Fetch the latest 15-minute candles.
  2. Update every open position: stop-loss first, then take-profit, using current price plus the latest candle high/low. Never use a candle that started before the position opened.
  3. If the 15-minute candle is new, or the scan was forced: fetch 4H and 1H candles, run the confluence analyzer, store a ScanResult with the blocker or score, and, if qualified, store a Signal, run risk checks, and open a trade.
  4. Snapshot equity every five minutes.
  5. Send Telegram notifications for entries, exits, rejections, and errors.

Duplicate protection

  • One signal per (symbol, side, candle timestamp).
  • One open position per symbol, plus a global maximum.
  • An in-process scan lock prevents overlapping scans.

Trading strategy

Classification

Multi-timeframe, trend-following continuation with SMC/price-action confluence and a fixed-R risk model. It combines a higher-timeframe EMA trend filter, market structure (BOS/CHoCH), order blocks and fair value gaps, a pullback entry, ATR-based stops, a 2R fixed target, and volume and momentum confirmation. It is not pure SMC, mean reversion, breakout-only, scalping, or market making.

Decision pipeline

Every item must be true before a trade is eligible.

  1. 4H trend filter. Bullish: EMA50 > EMA200 and price > EMA50. Bearish: the mirror. Mixed means no trade.
  2. 1H structure filter. The latest confirmed 1H BOS/CHoCH must agree with the 4H direction and be no older than 30 bars. 1H EMA20/EMA50 must agree for the momentum score.
  3. 1H zone context. Price must be within 2.0 ATR of an active, unmitigated 1H order block or FVG to earn the zone component.
  4. 15M execution trigger. A fresh 15M BOS/CHoCH in the trend direction within 4 bars. Price must have pulled back into a 15M OB/FVG or the 15M EMA20, and must not be more than 2.8 ATR from the 15M EMA20. Volume should be at least 1.1× the 20-bar average for the volume component.
  5. Confluence threshold. Minimum score defaults to 70/100. Lower setups are recorded as below-threshold and not executed.
  6. Risk and execution gates. Paused state, duplicate symbol, max positions, daily loss, loss streak, stop width, minimum notional, and leverage cap are checked. Live mode also needs wallet equity from MEXC, the environment master switch, and dashboard arming.

Stop, target, and position size

Stop (long; short is mirrored)

  • Candidates: recent swing low, nearest active zone bottom, lowest low of the last six entry candles, and entry − 0.8 ATR.
  • The stop sits beyond the lowest candidate with a 0.35 ATR buffer.
  • If the distance exceeds 4 ATR, it is capped at 3 ATR. If it is tighter than 0.35 ATR, it is widened to 0.6 ATR.

Target

entry ± 2R by default, where R is the stop distance. The target is fixed: there is no trailing stop or partial exit in the current engine.

Size

risk_amount = equity × risk_pct
qty         = risk_amount / stop_distance
qty         = min(qty, remaining_notional_capacity / entry)
notional    = qty × entry

The notional cap is equity × leverage × 0.95 minus existing open notional. Leverage is a notional cap, not a multiplier on the allowed loss.

Position lifecycle

  1. Signal is written with status new.
  2. Risk engine returns allowed or a rejection reason.
  3. Paper or MEXC broker returns a fill.
  4. Trade is stored as open with entry, stop, target, fees, leverage, and mode.
  5. Every scan checks current price and the latest 15M high/low.
  6. Exit reason is recorded: stop-loss, take-profit, or manual/kill-switch.
  7. PnL, fees, and R multiple are stored, and Telegram is notified.
  8. Equity is snapshotted.

What the strategy is not

  • Not an AI/LLM system. /api/v1/signals/ai returns 501.
  • Not calendar-based. The engine does not know whether it is Monday, month-end, or the first trading day of the month.
  • Not a market maker, arbitrage, grid, or martingale system.
  • Not high-frequency. It evaluates closed 15-minute candles and polls every 20 seconds.
  • Not safe by default for large live size. Stops are monitored by the Python process, not resting on the exchange.

Backtesting

The backtester replays the same 4H/1H/15M rules with these approximations:

  • Enters at the next 15M open after a qualified signal.
  • Applies entry and exit slippage and fees.
  • Checks the stop before the target when both are touched in one candle.
  • Exits after 96 bars (24 hours) if neither level is hit.
  • One position at a time.
  • Ignores funding, liquidation, partial fills, queue position, and exchange outages.

It is useful for rejecting bad ideas, not for proving a live edge.

Operations, backup, and observability

  • APScheduler runs scans, the daily report, and the SQLite backup. Job runs keep retry counts and status history.
  • /metrics exposes Prometheus text; /api/v1/metrics exposes operational counters.
  • Backups use the SQLite online backup API with a retention window.
  • Deploy as a Windows process, NSSM service, systemd unit, or Docker/Caddy. For live trading, use HTTPS, a strong password, and ideally a VPN or access proxy.

Before increasing size, require

  • 3–5 years of data across multiple regimes.
  • BTC, ETH, SOL, and the configured non-crypto proxies.
  • Walk-forward or out-of-sample windows.
  • Fees, slippage, funding, and conservative fill assumptions.
  • At least 100 closed trades in the final validation sample.
  • Stable expectancy across symbols and periods, not one lucky month.

Profit factor alone is not enough. One outlier trade can produce a high profit factor alongside unacceptable drawdown or tail risk.

Calendar and seasonality

The key distinction

A calendar date does not cause a price move. Calendar effects are proxies for real flows: salary and pension inflows, fund rebalancing, futures and options expiry, CME and equity market opens, ETF creation and redemption, perpetual funding timestamps, holidays, macro releases, and thinner weekend staffing. Crypto trades 24/7, so the equity plumbing behind month-end and weekday effects is weaker. The liquidity and volatility pattern is far more reliable than the direction of returns.

Turn-of-month (TOM)

Common windows: last trading day plus the first three of the next month (−1, +3), or last two plus first two (−2, +2).

  • Some studies found positive TOM returns in BTC/ETH/LTC in samples ending around 2021.
  • A broad study of 22 calendar effects found few equity-style anomalies in crypto.
  • The effect has not been stable across later periods, definitions, and costs.
  • A 2026 practitioner replication found the BTC/ETH TOM premium concentrated before 2024 and negative in 2024–2026.
  • Even with positive TOM returns, a TOM-only strategy often fails to beat buy-and-hold after costs.

Verdict. An interesting hypothesis, not a proven standalone edge. Do not hard-code it into the trend system without a fresh out-of-sample test.

Weekdays and weekends

  • Older BTC samples (about 2013–2017) showed a positive Monday effect; later research found it did not persist after 2015.
  • Other studies found no classical day-of-week effect in returns, but lower weekend volatility and higher early-week volatility.
  • Volume and liquidity are consistently lower on weekends and local nights.
  • Weekend return direction is too unstable for “buy Friday” or “sell Monday” rules.

For this system: do not use a fixed weekday direction. Use weekday and liquidity data for execution risk (wider spreads, thinner books, more slippage, gap risk). If you test a weekend filter, make it a new-entry pause or a risk reduction, not a long/short signal. For NAS100 and XAU, the underlying cash market is closed on weekends even though the MEXC perpetual trades, so treat weekend price action as a separate regime.

Months of the year

January effect, “sell in May,” September weakness, and quarter-end rebalancing are equity folklore. Crypto evidence is mixed and regime-dependent, and does not hold consistently across coins, periods, and costs. Do not build the core system around month-of-year seasonality; use macro and event awareness instead.

Intraday sessions

  • Activity, volatility, and illiquidity rise into the London/New York overlap.
  • A large multi-exchange study found activity peaking around 16:00–17:00 UTC.
  • Returns and activity are typically below average roughly 01:00–05:00 UTC.
  • Bitcoin price discovery concentrates in London/New York overlapping hours.

For a 15-minute strategy, thin books mean stops can be hit by noise and market orders can fill badly. The session filter is currently off; if enabled it uses UTC hours and supports overnight windows. The default 07:00–21:00 UTC is broad, not optimized.

Recommended test: compare 24/7 behavior with a 12:00–18:00 UTC entry window and a weekday-only variant. Keep exits active 24/7. Do not assume the filter helps until out-of-sample expectancy improves after costs.

Date-handling rules for this codebase

  1. Use UTC for crypto. All candles and scheduler timestamps are UTC.
  2. Define “month” explicitly. For crypto, month start is 00:00 UTC on day one. For an index, use the exchange calendar, holidays, and half-days.
  3. Do not mix calendar days and trading days. For 24/7 crypto, TOM (−1, +3) effectively means calendar days.
  4. Account for daylight saving. London and New York session boundaries shift against UTC; current session hours are fixed UTC.
  5. Record timezone and session on every signal and trade.
  6. Treat month-end funding and expiry separately from the date label.
  7. Use the underlying market calendar for NAS100/XAU.

Recommended order of use

  1. Execution and liquidity filter: avoid thin weekend and night entries; widen slippage assumptions there.
  2. Risk overlay: reduce size around macro events, quarter-end, and expiry windows.
  3. Diagnostics: tag every signal with weekday, day-of-month, TOM flag, session, and volatility regime.
  4. Directional calendar edge, only after out-of-sample validation.

Do not add a hard “long at month-end” rule to a trend-continuation system without testing it. A calendar long bias and a 4H/1H pullback trigger are different strategies, and mixing them can shrink the sample and hide the real edge.

Calendar test plan

  1. Add metadata to signals: weekday, day_of_month, is_tom, session, atr_percentile, days_to_month_end.
  2. Backtest 3–5 years on every configured symbol.
  3. Per bucket, compute trade count, win rate, average R, expectancy, profit factor, max drawdown, and average slippage.
  4. Compare TOM windows with randomly placed four-day windows in the same months to control for trend and volatility clustering.
  5. Use walk-forward splits and a permutation or bootstrap test.
  6. Correct for multiple testing. Testing many weekdays, months, and windows guarantees false discoveries otherwise.
  7. Promote a rule only if it is stable across symbols, eras, and cost assumptions. Otherwise keep it as a diagnostic or risk overlay.

Strategy roadmap

There is no universal most-profitable strategy

Profitability depends on market and regime, holding period, costs and funding, liquidity and order size, leverage, drawdown tolerance, data and infrastructure, taxes, and reliability. A 70% win rate loses money if losses are larger than wins. A 35% win rate can be profitable with a real 2R payoff and controlled costs. A strong backtest can still fail live through overfitting, slippage, or regime change.

Recommendation

Keep the trend-continuation architecture and improve regime, volatility, and exit logic before adding a new entry indicator. Priority order:

PriorityWorkWhat to do
0Prove or reject the current edgeRun 3–5 years across BTC, ETH, SOL, NAS100, XAU with walk-forward windows and realistic funding and slippage. Report long and short separately. Do not change parameters before you have a baseline.
1Higher-timeframe regime filterTest daily price vs 1D EMA200, 1D EMA50/EMA200 alignment, 1D slope or 30-day return sign, realized-volatility percentile. A filter, not a new entry trigger.
2Volatility targeting and exitsSize inversely to ATR; partial profit at 1R with a runner to 3R; ATR trail after +1R; time stop earlier than 96 bars; reduce size around macro events. The fixed 2R target may be leaving trend on the table, or it may be the only thing keeping expectancy positive. Test it.
3Session and liquidity filterTest 12:00–18:00 UTC entries and a weekday-only variant, with exits live 24/7. Most evidence-backed calendar-like change, because it affects fills, slippage, and stop noise.
4Calendar overlay (optional)Test TOM (−2, +2) or (−1, +3) only after the above, as a small risk overlay or separate low-frequency book.
5Second, uncorrelated strategyOnly after the first is proven. Long-only time-series momentum with volatility scaling on liquid crypto, run as a separate book with its own risk budget. Mean reversion needs separate tail-risk controls.

If you want one named strategy

The most defensible research family for a retail crypto futures account is volatility-scaled, long-biased time-series momentum with a trend filter and strict risk control. It has broad cross-asset evidence, documented crypto results, historically stronger long-only than short-only performance, and volatility scaling limits the damage from any single high-volatility period. The evidence is not unanimous: some recent studies find raw crypto momentum insignificant after costs and after 2020. It is the best-supported family to test, not a promise.

Acceptance criteria

Keep a variant only if all of these hold:

  • Net expectancy is positive after fees, slippage, funding, and conservative fills.
  • Profit factor is comfortably above 1.0 (target ≥ 1.3, not a guarantee).
  • Max drawdown stays inside the account’s survival limit.
  • Results hold in walk-forward and out-of-sample windows.
  • Results hold across at least three symbols or two regimes.
  • At least 100 closed trades.
  • The gain is not driven by one month, one trade, or one parameter value.
  • The logic is simple enough to explain and monitor.
  • The live vs paper difference is measured, not assumed.

What not to do

  • Do not optimize the confluence threshold on the same data used to judge it.
  • Do not add many calendar filters and keep the best-looking combination.
  • Do not raise leverage to make a weak edge look profitable.
  • Do not use martingale, grid averaging, or “no stop” recovery logic.
  • Do not go live because a backtest shows a high win rate.
  • Do not assume a 24/7 crypto market has the same month-end flows as equities.

Operations

Configuration layers

  • .env: secrets, host/port, database URL, Telegram token, MEXC keys, live master switch, scheduler cadence, JWT secret, backup settings.
  • SQLite bot_settings: symbols, timeframes, risk, score threshold, leverage, session filter, execution mode, Telegram chat ID.
  • Dashboard and Telegram edit the database settings with no restart. Environment changes need a restart.

Safe operating sequence

  1. Run in paper mode.
  2. Confirm market data and scans.
  3. Backtest across symbols and regimes.
  4. Paper trade at least four weeks without changing rules.
  5. Compare paper results with the backtest.
  6. Go live only with the smallest MEXC size and LIVE_TRADING_ALLOWED=true.
  7. Arm live mode. The bot starts paused.
  8. Verify symbol, leverage, stop, and size before resuming.
  9. Use a sub-account, isolated margin, one-way mode, and no withdrawal key.
  10. Write down a stop-trading rule, for example −10% account drawdown.

Emergency actions

  • Pause: stops new entries; existing positions remain managed.
  • Kill switch: closes all positions and pauses.
  • Process down: close positions manually on MEXC. The bot places no resting exchange-side stops.
  • After an incident: reconcile the local journal against the exchange before resuming.

Backups

Security and limitations

Controls present

  • bcrypt password hashing; cookie sessions; CSRF and origin protection on state-changing paths.
  • Rate limiting and security headers (CSP, frame denial, no-sniff).
  • JWT support for API and WebSocket clients; optional API bearer token.
  • Telegram chat-ID allowlist.
  • MEXC live mode requires two explicit switches.
  • Secrets live in .env, not the repository.
  • Google OAuth uses a single-use expiring state and a verified email.
  • Telegram widget uses HMAC verification with the bot token and a freshness window.

Important limitations

  1. Software stops. If the process, VPS, or network dies, no exchange stop is resting.
  2. Single process. In-memory sessions and SQLite assume one worker; more would break sessions, the scheduler, and the scan lock.
  3. No funding or liquidation model. Backtests and paper PnL ignore funding, liquidation, and margin calls.
  4. No partial-fill or queue modeling. Live market orders fill differently.
  5. No funding-rate or reconciliation loop. The local journal can drift from the exchange.
  6. No exchange-side trailing stop. Target is fixed at 2R; stop is structural.
  7. Session filter is fixed UTC. No daylight-saving adjustment.
  8. Approximate backtest. One position at a time, no portfolio capital competition, no margin engine.
  9. No AI strategy. The AI endpoint is a placeholder.
  10. Social login needs configuration. Missing config hides the button.
  11. Market-data dependency. ccxt or REST outages and rate limits can delay scans and stop checks.
  12. No guarantee. Past performance and calendar studies do not imply future returns.

CHoCH
Change of Character; the first structural break against the prior direction.
FVG
Fair Value Gap; a three-candle price imbalance.
OB
Order Block; the last opposing candle before a structural break.
R
Initial stop distance; a 2R target is twice the stop distance.
Expectancy
Average profit or loss per trade after costs.
Profit factor
Gross profit ÷ gross loss.
TOM
Turn of the month.
TSMOM
Time-series momentum; an asset’s own trailing return used as the signal.
Walk-forward
Repeatedly optimizing on an in-sample window and testing on the next unseen window.
Out-of-sample
Data not used to choose the rules or parameters.
